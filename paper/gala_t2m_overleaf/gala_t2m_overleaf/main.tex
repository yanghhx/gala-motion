\begin{abstract}
Text-to-motion generation must couple language semantics with skeletal kinematics, yet most latent generators use pooled text conditioning or denoise raw poses at high cost.
Existing methods lack an explicit bridge between skeletal topology, fine-grained semantics, and the generative trajectory.
GALA establishes that bridge: a skeleton-graph VAE exposes five body-part latents; learnable anatomical queries align CLIP tokens to those parts; a rectified-flow DiT then transports noise under motion-space kinematic constraints, sampled with a 20--50 step Euler ODE.
Under the official 20-replication protocol, GALA reaches R@3 $0.806$ / FID $0.272$ at 20 NFEs and FID $0.224$ at 50 NFEs on HumanML3D; on KIT-ML it obtains R@3 $0.804$ / FID $0.402$.
The graph tokenizer reconstructs at FID $0.003$, while part indexing and kinematic supervision reduce 20-NFE generation FID from $0.337$ to $0.272$.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
text-to-motion, rectified flow, skeleton graph, cross-modal alignment, human motion generation
\end{keywords}

\section{Introduction}

Text-to-motion (T2M) maps a sentence to a variable-length pose clip~\cite{guo2022humanml3d,tevet2023mdm}.
HumanML3D~\cite{guo2022humanml3d} and KIT-ML~\cite{plappert2016kit} are the standard splits; the Guo protocol---R-Precision, FID~\cite{heusel2017fid}, multimodal distance, Diversity, 20 replications---is the number reviewers compare~\cite{guo2022humanml3d,tevet2023mdm}.
Diffusion on raw $263$-D poses~\cite{tevet2023mdm,zhang2022motiondiffuse} is faithful but slow; latent diffusion~\cite{chen2023mld} and discrete tokens~\cite{zhang2023t2mgpt,guo2024momask,kong2023m2dm} push FID down at the cost of a codebook or a long denoising chain.
Rectified flow~\cite{liu2023rectified,lipman2023flow} replaces that chain with a direct ODE trajectory and enables low-NFE continuous generation.

A topology-aware variational tokenizer already yields a compact, near-lossless motion latent, so generation reduces to learning a \emph{straight} path in that space that is explicitly aligned with language.
Existing latent T2M methods lack an explicit structural bridge between skeletal topology, fine-grained language semantics, and the generative trajectory.
GALA establishes this bridge by linking three stages that are not interchangeable: the graph tokenizer exists to expose anatomical parts; part-level alignment exists so language can address those parts; kinematic flow exists because reconstruction FID shows that fidelity is lost in the ODE, not in the VAE---skeleton graph $\to$ part latents $\leftrightarrow$ language tokens $\to$ kinematic rectified flow.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Anatomy-aware motion tokenizer.} A CTR graph encoder plus stride-4 VAE maps $263$-D poses to $256$-D latents at 5\,Hz, preserving skeletal dependencies and exposing part-structured features. Val reconstruction FID is $0.003$.
\item \textbf{Part-indexed language--motion alignment.} Beyond pooled InfoNCE, five fixed-index queries condition the flow and are supervised by corresponding graph-pooled body parts; a collapse audit verifies that their attention profiles remain distinct.
\item \textbf{Kinematic-aware rectified flow.} An 8-block DiT learns a straight latent ODE under classifier-free guidance~\cite{ho2022cfg}, with velocity, bone-length, and foot-contact penalties in decoded motion space, enabling 20--50 NFE sampling.
\end{enumerate}

We compare against native-HumanML3D generators (MDM, MLD, M2DM, T2M-GPT, EMDM, MoMask) and treat GENMO~\cite{li2025genmo} as one generalist SMPL baseline, not as the target. Code is available at \href{https://github.com/yanghhx/gala-motion}{github.com/yanghhx/gala-motion}.

\section{Related work}

Pose-space DDPMs~\cite{tevet2023mdm,zhang2022motiondiffuse,ho2020ddpm} are faithful but slow.
MLD~\cite{chen2023mld} diffuses in a VAE latent---the same split we use, without a skeleton graph or a straight flow.
T2M-GPT~\cite{zhang2023t2mgpt}, M2DM~\cite{kong2023m2dm}, MoMask~\cite{guo2024momask}, and MMM~\cite{pinyoanuntapong2024mmm} quantize motion; we stay continuous because a 256-D VAE already reconstructs at FID $0.003$.
Consistency models~\cite{dai2024motionlcm} and EMDM~\cite{zhou2024emdm} cut the denoising chain; rectified flow~\cite{liu2023rectified,lipman2023flow} learns a direct path that a DiT~\cite{peebles2023dit} can parameterize under CFG~\cite{ho2022cfg}.
FlowMotion~\cite{cuba2025flowmotion} predicts the clean motion in conditional flow matching, MotionFlux~\cite{gao2025motionflux} combines rectified flow with preference alignment, and MotionHiFlow~\cite{li2026motionhiflow} builds a hierarchical temporal flow with topology-aware encoding. Thus, flow matching itself is not our novelty: GALA fixes anatomical query-to-part identities, uses the resulting part tokens as DiT conditions, and supervises the flow endpoint with decoded-space kinematics.
ST-GCN~\cite{yan2018stgcn} and CTR-GCN~\cite{chen2021ctrgcn} supply the topology prior.
Part-level T2M~\cite{zou2024parco,zhong2023att2m} generates or attends over limbs; GALA instead aligns language tokens to part latents inside one continuous flow.
GENMO~\cite{li2025genmo} is a generalist SMPL~\cite{loper2015smpl} model whose HumanML3D numbers pass through a conversion the authors call an inherent disadvantage; we list it as a reference, not as the target.

\section{Method}

\noindent\textbf{Representation.}
A clip is $x_{1:T}\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times 263}$, $T\le 196$, 20\,fps, Guo features~\cite{guo2022humanml3d}: root motion, local joints, velocities, and four foot-contact channels.
KIT-ML uses the analogous $251$-D, $21$-joint feature.
Text is frozen CLIP ViT-B/32~\cite{radford2021clip} tokens $c_{1:N}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times 512}$.

\noindent\textbf{Part-aware graph tokenizer.}
Local joint positions and velocities form a $J$-node graph.
Two CTR blocks mix a static anatomical adjacency with a sample-dependent attention graph; an ST-GCN variant (static adjacency only) and a graph-free conv VAE are the tokenizer controls.
Joints are pooled into $P{=}5$ parts
$\mathcal{P}=\{\text{torso},\,\text{L-arm},\,\text{R-arm},\,\text{L-leg},\,\text{R-leg}\}$,
giving a global frame feature $z_g$ and part features $z_p\in\mathbb{R}^{P\times d}$.
A 1-D conv VAE~\cite{kingma2014vae} with stride 4 emits $\mu,\log\sigma^2\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times 256}$, $L=\lceil T/4\rceil$.
Stage-1 training is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{VAE}}
=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{KL}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KL}}
+\lambda_{\mathrm{vel}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{vel}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{bone}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{bone}}.
\end{equation}
After 35k AdamW steps the tokenizer is frozen.

\noindent\textbf{Fine-grained language--motion alignment.}
Global alignment remains symmetric InfoNCE on pooled CLIP and pooled latents,
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{global}}=\mathrm{InfoNCE}(\bar c,\bar z)$, weight $0.25$.
On top of that, $P$ learnable anatomical queries $q_p$ attend to CLIP tokens,
\begin{equation}
\tilde c_p=\mathrm{Attn}(q_p,\,c_{1:N}),
\end{equation}
and are trained to match the corresponding part latent
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{part}}
=\frac1P\sum_{p=1}^{P}\mathrm{SymNCE}
\left(\{z_{b,p}\}_{b=1}^{B},\{\tilde c_{b,p}\}_{b=1}^{B}\right),
\end{equation}
where negatives are other examples in the minibatch. Each query is contrasted only with the graph-pooled latent at the same ordered index: torso, left arm, right arm, left leg, and right leg. This predefined correspondence prevents permutation ambiguity among the five slots.
The same $\tilde c_p$ are concatenated to CLIP tokens as extra DiT context, so the branch is used at sampling time rather than only as a projection head. We do not equate attention weights with explanations: the diagnostic below establishes non-collapse, while the fixed-index contrastive targets provide anatomical supervision.

\noindent\textbf{Kinematic rectified flow.}
Given clean $\mu$ (detached), $t\sim\mathcal{U}(0,1)$, $\varepsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$,
\begin{equation}
z_t=(1-t)\varepsilon+t\,\mu,\qquad v^\star=\mu-\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
An 8-block DiT (width 512, 8 heads) predicts $v_\theta(z_t,t,c)$ with AdaLN modulation on the pooled text vector, self-attention on latent tokens, and cross-attention to CLIP$+\,$part tokens.
Condition dropout $0.1$ enables CFG~\cite{ho2022cfg}.
The flow loss is $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RF}}=\|v_\theta-v^\star\|_2^2$.
We additionally decode the endpoint estimate $\hat z_1=z_t+(1-t)v_\theta$ with the frozen decoder and penalize motion-space kinematics:
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{vel}}
&=\tfrac1{T-1}\sum_t\|(\hat x_{t+1}-\hat x_t)-(x_{t+1}-x_t)\|_1,\\
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{bone}}
&=\sum_{(i,j)\in E}\bigl|\|\hat p_i-\hat p_j\|_2-\|p_i-p_j\|_2\bigr|,
\end{align}
plus an $\ell_1$ term $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{foot}}$ on the four foot-contact channels.
The generator objective is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}={}&\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RF}}
+\lambda_g\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{global}}
+\lambda_p\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{part}}\\
&+\lambda_v\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{vel}}
+\lambda_b\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{bone}}
+\lambda_f\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{foot}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Sampling is Euler,
$z\leftarrow z+\tfrac1N\bigl(v_\emptyset+\omega(v_c-v_\emptyset)\bigr)$,
with $N\in\{10,20,50\}$ and $\omega\in\{2.0,2.5\}$.

\noindent\textbf{Contact-aware skating loss.}
The existing $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{foot}}$ reconstructs the four GT foot-contact channels but does not penalize physical skating.
We add a contact-aware skating loss on the decoded endpoint $\hat X=D(\hat z_1)$.
World-frame foot positions $\hat p_{t,f}$ are recovered from the 263-D Guo features via \texttt{recover\_ric}.
The GT contact mask $c_{t,f}$ is taken from the last four binary channels (GT-contact-conditioned generated displacement):
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{skate}}=\frac{\sum_{t,f}c_{t,f}\|\hat p_{t+1,f}-\hat p_{t,f}\|_2^2}{\sum_{t,f}c_{t,f}+\epsilon}.
\end{equation}
Because $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{skate}}{\sim}10^{-4}$ while $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RF}}{\sim}60$, a fixed weight would need $\lambda{\sim}3{\times}10^5$.
We instead use \emph{adaptive weighting} $\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}=\eta\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RF}}/\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{skate}}$ so that $\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{skate}}/\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RF}}\approx\eta$ automatically.
A decay schedule $\eta{:}0.03{\to}0.01$ over the first 40k steps recovers FID while retaining skating reduction.

\noindent\textbf{Training.}
Stage 1: VAE only, 35k steps, effective batch 16, bf16.
Stage 2: freeze graph$+$VAE, train flow$+$alignment, 100k steps, lr $2{\times}10^{-4}$, EMA $0.9999$.
The graph+global control in Table~\ref{tab:main} uses the same tokenizer and DiT budget as the full model. Full GALA adds part alignment and decoded-space kinematic supervision.

\subsection{HumanML3D}

Fig.~\ref{fig:compare} and Table~\ref{tab:main} share one reading.

\textbf{Semantic metrics at low NFE.}
At 20 NFEs GALA reaches R@3 $0.806{\pm.002}$ and MM Dist $3.061{\pm.009}$, improving the graph+global control by $0.038$ and $0.187$, respectively. It matches MoMask's reported R@3 ($0.807$) within uncertainty while using a continuous 20-step trajectory. R@1 / R@2 are $0.494{\pm.003}$ / $0.701{\pm.002}$; Diversity $9.69$ remains close to Real $9.50$.

\textbf{FID is the remaining gap, and it is a generator gap.}
GALA's 50-NFE FID $0.224$ improves the graph+global control ($0.312$) and M2DM ($0.352$), but trails T2M-GPT / EMDM / MoMask ($0.141$ / $0.112$ / $0.045$).
We do not claim SOTA fidelity.
Val reconstruction FID is $0.003$: the Guo extractor barely distinguishes reconstructed clips from real ones, so the residual $0.224$ generated FID is a generator rather than tokenizer bottleneck.

GENMO's SMPL$\leftrightarrow$HumanML3D conversion makes its FID $0.216$ a weak target for a native-HML3D model. We report it for completeness and do not organize the paper around beating it.

\section{Conclusion}

GALA links graph-derived part latents, part-indexed language alignment, and a kinematically supervised rectified flow. Official 20-replication experiments show that the complete chain improves 20-NFE FID from $0.402$ to $0.272$ and R@3 from $0.789$ to $0.806$, while 50 NFEs reach FID $0.224$. The tokenizer is near-lossless, so closing the remaining fidelity gap calls for a stronger transport model rather than a larger codebook.
